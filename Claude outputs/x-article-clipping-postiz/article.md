## 1. find content that's already working

clipping starts with other people's content, so the first job is finding what already proved it can hold attention. nobody in this system starts from a blank page, me included.

my clippers look in three places. youtube long form in the niche, meaning podcasts and interviews sorted by views, because one good hour can hold a dozen clips. tiktok search for niche terms, filtered by most liked in the past month, because a format that went viral a year ago is probably saturated by now. and x advanced search with min_faves:500 or higher, which surfaces the takes and screenshots people are already arguing about.

the rule I care about most is repetition. one viral video can be luck or timing, so I only get excited when the same format works across several accounts, since that means the format is doing the work and it will probably work for my app too.

every source also has to pass one filter before it goes anywhere: the creator said yes, the podcast actively invites clippers, or there's a usage deal. that list lives in the whop brief, and nothing outside it gets clipped.

## 2. let opus 5.5 find the moments

this is where most of the time used to go. a clipper would watch an hour of podcast to find two usable moments, and half the time they'd pick ones that only make sense with context from earlier in the episode.

now they paste the transcript into opus 5.5 along with the top comments and ask for the moments that stand on their own. youtube gives you the transcript for free, and the comments are the underrated part, because the most liked ones tell you what people actually reacted to.

this is the prompt my clippers use:

> "here's the transcript of a [length] podcast in the peptide / longevity niche with timestamps, plus the 20 most-liked comments. find the 5 moments that work as standalone 20 to 40 second clips. for each one give me the timestamps, the exact opening line, why someone stops scrolling, the payoff, and where my app fits as one natural line near the end. skip anything that needs earlier context."

look at the last line. opus 5.5 read the comments, noticed that about a third of them ask how the guest kept track, and told the clipper which clip to post first, which is something you'd never catch skimming the video at 2x.

## 3. cut the clip

a clip only earns distribution when it adds something new, and both big platforms tightened this up in 2026. tiktok's guidelines, which took effect last week, say you should only post content you created or have the right to share, and reused material with nothing new added isn't eligible for the for you feed. instagram said in may that accounts which mostly share unoriginal photos, videos or carousels drop out of recommendations.

[image: images/05-rules-tiktok-instagram.png]

so every clip needs two things: permission, which the source list handles, and a real edit on top. here's what a clip that clears review looks like:

[image: images/04-clip-anatomy.png]

the strongest line of the conversation moves to the first two seconds with text on screen, the middle keeps one idea and cuts everything else, captions stay on the whole time, and the app gets one natural line near the end, the kind a real person would say. the creator gets credited in the caption, and anything with someone else's watermark gets rejected.

## 4. test new concepts with opus 5.5

clipping covers most of the volume, but sometimes a clipper wants to test an angle nobody has made yet, and that's the second job opus 5.5 does for them: it turns proven formats into new concepts, then produces the slides.

the key is feeding it real examples. asking for "20 viral ideas" gets you the same generic list everyone else gets, so the clipper pastes 5 to 10 posts that performed in the niche this month and asks for the mechanism behind them.

> "here are 8 slideshows and clips that performed in the peptide niche this month, with hooks, slide text and top comments. analyze them like a content strategist: find the pattern (hook, curiosity gap, pacing, payoff, where the product shows up). then give me 20 new concepts with the same mechanism in completely different scenarios. no health claims, no dosing talk, and the app only shows up as the tool the narrator used."

[image: images/06-opus-pattern-and-concepts.png]

then it builds the slides. opus 5.5 makes visuals by writing code, and that turns out to be perfect for slideshows. anything that's a phone screen, like a lock screen notification, a notes page or a timeline, gets built in code, so every pixel of the ui is exact and you skip the rounds of fixing notification sizes and spacing you get with an image model. the photo slides get a prompt each, and the clipper generates them through an image connector in the same chat.

since those photos are realistic and ai-generated, tiktok wants the ai-generated label on them, so that's in the brief too. if the test clears the whop bar, it goes to the agency and they build variations around it.

## 5. hand it to the clippers: whop content rewards

[image: images/09-whop-create-content-reward.png]

a campaign takes a few minutes to set up. you pick clipping or ugc, a category, a total budget, the rate per 1,000 views, a minimum and maximum payout per video and the platforms you accept (tiktok, youtube shorts, x and instagram reels), then you attach a doc with your guidelines and the source list. the current terms put the minimum budget at $1,000 per campaign, and approved payouts settle about ten days after approval.

[image: images/10-whop-setup.gif]

the brief is where most campaigns fail. clippers optimize for views, so every requirement has to make sure your app lives inside those views, and every requirement has to be something a moderator can check in five seconds.

this is roughly the brief I run:

> clips cut only from the source list in this doc, or slideshows of 4 to 8 slides.
> the app shows up inside the story: a notification, a screen, a line of voiceover. it never lives only on the last frame.
> videos under 1,000 views don't get paid.
> at least 60% of the audience in tier 1 english-speaking countries.
> branded content toggle on for every post, and the ai-generated label on any slide with ai photos.
> credit the original creator in the caption.
> no dosing advice, no before/after body shots, no "cured" or "fixed" language.
> no watermarks, no reuploads without a real edit.

two of those lines were part of the deal I agreed on before launch, and they do most of the work. the first is that no video under 1,000 views gets paid, because a clip that dies under 1k is usually shadowbanned or just bad, and without that floor you end up paying for a pile of posts nobody saw.

the second is a guarantee that at least 60% of the audience on every paid video sits in tier 1 english-speaking countries like the usa, uk, canada and australia. that's where subscription revenue actually comes from for most apps, and a clip that blows up somewhere else can bring a million views and close to zero revenue.

I also cap the max payout per video, so one clip that goes accidentally viral can't eat the whole budget in a day.

the math is what sold me on paying per view. contentstudio's 2026 benchmarks put the average meta cpm around $14, and health and wellness runs $15 to $22. at $2 per 1,000 views, a million views on whop costs about $2,200 with the 10% platform fee, and while impressions and views aren't the same unit, you can fudge that gap a lot and still land somewhere between 7x and 10x cheaper.

## 6. the agency that only clips peptide apps

content rewards gives me volume and a lot of randomness, and the agency is where the consistency comes from.

a niche agency already knows which phrasing gets a video pulled in this space, runs accounts that are warmed up for the niche, and has seen what converts for other peptide apps before it ever touches yours. that's knowledge you'd otherwise pay for with months of removed posts.

it costs more upfront than a content rewards campaign, so I don't ask them to guess. the formats that performed on whop go to them, they can run the same opus 5.5 prompts to pull moments and build variations, and they turn the winners into steady daily posts.

## 7. organize and schedule everything with postiz

once clippers, an agency and your own accounts are all shipping, the hard part moves to keeping track of what goes out where, when and in which format. a spreadsheet and a phone hold up for about a week, then posts get doubled, forgotten or go out at 3am on the wrong account.

this is where I connect opus 5.5 to [postiz](https://postiz.com). postiz is in the claude connector directory, so the connection is an oauth sign-in with no api key, and in claude code it's one command:

```
claude mcp add --transport http postiz https://mcp.postiz.com/mcp/your-api-key
```

once it's connected, opus 5.5 can see every channel, create drafts and schedule posts, and if you also give it the postiz skill with `npx skills add gitroomhq/postiz-agent`, it can upload the finished mp4s itself. on sunday I hand it the whole week in one message:

> "here are 14 slideshows and 10 clips for next week. spread them across the in-house group, max 2 posts per account per day. slideshows go to tiktok as drafts, clips go direct at 9am and 7pm. tag everything by format and save it all as drafts so I can review it before anything is scheduled."

a few details make this hold up at volume.

channels are grouped. postiz calls these customer groups, and I keep one for my in-house accounts and one for the agency's, so filtering the calendar to a single group shows me that week without the noise. if an agency runs accounts for you, send them an invite link from postiz: they connect the accounts on the platform's own screen and nobody shares a password.

every post gets a colored tag by format, clip or slideshow, plus one for "proven on whop" versus "new test", so one look at the week tells me if I'm leaning too hard on one format.

tiktok slideshows go out as real photo posts through the upload option, which drops each post into the tiktok app inbox as a draft. I add the trending sound and the ai label in the app and publish, and tiktok gives you 24 hours to do it. it's the one manual step left, because the api can auto-add music but has no way to pick the sound that's trending this week. clips go out as direct posts with the branded content toggle on.

nothing new goes out blind. the agency sends me a postiz preview link for any new format, I leave comments right on the post, and they move it from draft to scheduled once it's fixed.

## 8. double down on what works

when a format works, I don't move on to something new. I ask for five variations of the winner, same structure with a new scenario and a new first frame, and the formats that stall get killed after a week. decide your kill threshold before launch, so you're not negotiating with your own hopes on day six.

and... views are the cheap part now. if your install-to-paid is under 4%, a clipping budget just buys you more people who leave, so fix the paywall before you scale the campaign.

organic clip traffic also doesn't attribute cleanly, so add a "where did you hear about us?" question to your onboarding with tiktok, instagram and youtube as options. it's the only way to know which engine actually brings paying users.

ask yourself honestly how many videos about your app went up last week that you didn't make yourself. if the answer is zero, your distribution is capped by your own posting schedule.

once it's running, the loop is simple. the source list feeds the clippers, opus 5.5 finds the moments and turns winners into new concepts, whop pays only for approved videos that clear 1,000 views with a mostly tier 1 audience, and the agency turns the winners into steady daily posts on accounts built for this niche.

then the numbers feed next week's brief, and it starts again.

every post on accounts I control lands in one calendar, with drafts, groups, tags and opus 5.5 doing the scheduling, and that last layer is [postiz](https://postiz.com).
